Deep Dive

The first debt-relief product should be smaller than the old guide idea.

This got promoted because the money lane only helps if the product can be finished without turning into a giant risky handbook. The better first move is a smaller starter pack built from transferable coordination knowledge.

Why this matters

Faster to ship, easier to price, and much safer than packaging employer-specific material too closely.

What I did

I drafted a boundary note that separates good product angles from risky ones, then reframed the likely first offer as a field-to-office coordination starter pack instead of a giant all-in-one process encyclopedia.

What changed because of that work

The first monetization lane is now narrower in a useful way. It has a shape that is more likely to get shipped and less likely to overreach into sensitive company-specific material.

Best next move

1. Define modules

Outline the guide, checklist, glossary, and workflow map.

2. Keep it generic

Use abstracted process language, not internal project artifacts or screenshots.

3. Price modestly

Treat it as a starter product that proves shipping discipline.

4. Expand later

If it lands well, the broader guide can become a later version.
